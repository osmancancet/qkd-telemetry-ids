\section{Conclusion}

Count telemetry cannot certify the absence of a detector-control attack. A faked-state attacker who reproduces the honest photon-number yields and error probabilities leaves the joint law of all window counts unchanged, so every count-telemetry detector works at chance while the finite-key analysis outputs a key the attacker knows almost entirely. In our simulations the hypothesis was met closely enough by an attacker with an ordinary threshold detector, which only a post-hoc block-level statistic separated, and by an attacker who estimated the channel from five minutes of public counts, which no detector separated at any operating point. Against attacks inside the device model the decoy-state bound already protects the key, and detection serves operations rather than secrecy.

Between those cases lies the useful work of a monitor: catching attackers whose emulation is imperfect without raising alarms on a healthy, drifting link. In this simulation model, detectors that compare absolute levels with drift-free calibration data alarmed in most benign windows under nominal drift, and drift-calibrated learned detectors did not hold their target in either direction. Detectors built on a per-window refit of the honest channel held a false-alarm rate near $10^{-3}$ up to fourfold drift with drift-free calibration only, provided an intensity monitor is present (and, for the sequential forms, unbiased) and the residuals avoid basis-dependent efficiencies. They caught naive blinding at once and in-model attacks reliably; their sequential forms caught part of the error-free and background-free emulation; and none caught emulation whose parameters stay physically plausible. The simulations also showed that slow intensity drift invalidates the single-photon bound with nominal intensities, so per-window intensity monitoring is needed both for the key and for the monitor. The next steps are tests on recorded telemetry from an operating link, adaptive attackers, and monitors that combine count telemetry with timing and coincidence statistics. Code and per-seed results are released with this article.
