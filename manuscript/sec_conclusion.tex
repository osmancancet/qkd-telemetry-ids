\section{Conclusion}

Window telemetry cannot certify the absence of a detector-control attack. A faked-state attacker who reproduces the honest photon-number yields and error probabilities leaves the joint law of all counts unchanged, so every telemetry detector, learned or physical, works at chance while the finite-key analysis outputs a key the attacker knows. Against attacks inside the device model, the decoy-state bound already protects the key, and detection serves operations rather than secrecy.

Between those two cases lies the useful work of a monitor: catching attackers whose calibration is imperfect without raising alarms on a healthy, drifting link. Here the benchmark separates the detectors sharply. Classifiers and isolation forests calibrated on stationary data alarmed in more than 97\% of benign windows under nominal drift; domain randomisation reduced but did not remove the effect. A decoy-consistency statistic that refits the drifting parameters per window held the false-alarm rate near its $10^{-3}$ target up to fourfold drift and flagged naive and gain-only blinding in the first window, but it misses attacks that the refit absorbs. Future work should test the statistic on recorded telemetry from a field link, add sequential decision rules, and couple telemetry monitoring with hardware countermeasures that change what an attacker can reproduce. Code and per-seed results are released with this article, together with the manuscript source.
