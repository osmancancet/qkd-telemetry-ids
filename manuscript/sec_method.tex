\section{Decoy-consistency statistic}\label{sec:dcs}

Drift moves four physical parameters of the honest model: $\eta$, $Y_0$ (which also absorbs afterpulses, since they add a nearly constant background) and the misalignment in each basis. The count telemetry of one window has far more degrees of freedom. The decoy-consistency statistic (DCS) spends four of them on tracking the channel and tests the rest.

\paragraph{Per-window refit.} Let $Q_k=\sum_a n^{\rm all}_{a,k}/\sum_a N_{a,k}$ be the gain of intensity $k$ from all announced clicks, and use the monitored intensities $\hat\mu_k$. A fixed-point iteration on the signal and vacuum-like gains,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fit}
\hat\eta=-\frac{1}{\hat\mu_1}\ln\frac{1-Q_{\mu_1}}{1-\hat Y_0},\qquad
\hat Y_0=\frac{Q_{\mu_3}-\bigl(1-e^{-\hat\eta\hat\mu_3}\bigr)}{e^{-\hat\eta\hat\mu_3}},
\end{equation}
started from the linear approximation $\hat Y_0=Q_{\mu_3}-(\hat\mu_3/\hat\mu_1)Q_{\mu_1}$ and run for six steps, gives $(\hat\eta,\hat Y_0)$. The sifted signal error rate $E_{b}$ of each basis then gives $\hat e_d^b=(E_bQ_{\mu_1}-e_0\hat Y_0)/(Q_{\mu_1}-\hat Y_0)$, clipped to $[0,1/2]$.

\paragraph{Residuals.} With these estimates the honest model (\ref{eq:honest}) predicts the observations that were not used in the fit, and four binomial residuals
\begin{equation}
r_j=\frac{c_j-M_j\hat p_j}{\sqrt{M_j\hat p_j(1-\hat p_j)}},\qquad j=1,\dots,4,
\end{equation}
compare an observed count $c_j$ out of $M_j$ trials with its predicted probability $\hat p_j$. They are the decoy gain, the sifted decoy error rate in $Z$ and in $X$, and the vacuum-like error rate (bases pooled, with the mean of $\hat e_d^Z$ and $\hat e_d^X$). The decoy-state estimator uses the same relations to bound the single-photon contribution over a block; the DCS turns them into a per-window test with a controlled false-alarm rate. The afterpulse background is taken as constant across the three intensities within a window, which holds to first order because the intensities are interleaved pulse by pulse.

Three further residuals are available in principle: the sifting fraction of signal clicks in each of Alice's bases, which equals $p^B_a$ if Bob's click probability is independent of his basis choice, and the share of signal clicks on pulses Alice sent in $Z$, which equals the share of such pulses if the yield is independent of Alice's basis. They test the basis-independence hypothesis of Proposition~\ref{prop:indist} directly, but at $10^6$--$10^7$ clicks per window they resolve relative changes of order $10^{-3}$, below the slow drift of the efficiency of Bob's two measurement paths and of polarisation-dependent loss in deployed links. In our model with 0.5\% path-efficiency and 0.25\% polarisation-dependent-loss drift (Table~\ref{tab:params}) the seven-residual statistic (DCS-7) raised false alarms in \sensNomSevenFprOne\% of windows at $d=1$. We therefore report DCS-7 only as an ablation in Section~\ref{sec:sens}. A link whose basis-path efficiencies are stabilised or monitored to $10^{-3}$ could add these residuals back.

\paragraph{Statistic and threshold.} The statistic is
\begin{equation}
S_t=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\left(\frac{r_j-\mathrm{med}_j}{1.4826\,\mathrm{MAD}_j}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
with the median and median absolute deviation taken from one benign stream recorded without slow drift. The robust standardisation absorbs effects that inflate a residual beyond its binomial variance, such as the fast intensity noise that the monitor does not remove. An alarm is raised when $S_t$ exceeds the $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of $S$ on separate drift-free benign calibration windows. A sequential version accumulates evidence across windows with Page's CUSUM \cite{page1954}: $C_t=\max\{0,C_{t-1}+(S_t-\bar S)/s_S-k\}$ with $k=1/2$, where $\bar S$ and $s_S$ are the calibration mean and standard deviation, and $C_t$ is thresholded in the same way. A block version (DCS-block) averages the standardised residuals over the last $L=60$ windows (10 minutes), $\bar z_{j,t}=L^{-1}\sum_{s=t-L+1}^{t} (r_{j,s}-\mathrm{med}_j)/(1.4826\,\mathrm{MAD}_j)$, standardises each $\bar z_j$ again with the median and MAD of the same block means on the calibration stream (which absorbs their autocorrelation), and sums the squares; it targets small persistent shifts that a single window cannot resolve. We added it after the first round of review, in response to a small but systematic shift of the Blind-thresh decoy gain (Section~\ref{sec:indist-results}); it is therefore a post-hoc detector, and its thresholds and window length were fixed before the final runs.

\paragraph{Disclosure, cost and blind spots.} The DCS needs, per window, the basis and intensity of every pulse with its click outcome, which Alice and Bob reveal anyway during sifting and parameter estimation, and the error counts per basis and intensity. Errors in the test basis $X$ are compared publicly and are available in real time. Errors in the key basis $Z$ are known only after error correction; the DCS assumes that error correction runs on frames of one window (about $10^6$ sifted bits here, a common frame size), so its decisions lag the window by the error-correction latency, and Blind-lag is assumed to read the same per-window error rates from the public discussion. Where $Z$ errors are not available per window, the variant DCS-X fits only $\hat e_d^X$ and uses three residuals (decoy gain, decoy $X$ error rate and vacuum-like $X$ error rate); it is evaluated alongside the DCS. Scoring a single window costs about 0.1\,ms in our Python implementation (vectorised over a day, \dcsLatencyUs\,$\mu$s per window). Slow drift of $\eta$, $Y_0$, $e_d^Z$ or $e_d^X$ moves the estimates, not the residuals. The refit also defines what the DCS cannot see. Any attack whose window statistics coincide with an honest channel at \emph{some} parameter values passes unseen, whether or not those values are physically plausible: pure added loss looks like lower transmittance, a missing background looks like a lower $Y_0$, and error-free faked clicks look like zero misalignment. Section~\ref{sec:sens} tests whether plausibility floors on the fitted parameters (DCS+F) can close this gap; they cannot without false alarms on ordinary operational changes.
